Epigallocatechin-3-gallate inhibits alpha-glucosidase through a mixed-type mechanism: kinetic and molecular docking insights

Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors are widely used to control postprandial hyperglycemia in patients with type 2 diabetes. In this study, we investigated the inhibitory effect of epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG), the most abundant catechin in green tea, on alpha-glucosidase from Saccharomyces cerevisiae. EGCG inhibited the enzyme in a dose-dependent manner with an IC50 value of 12.4 micromolar, which was approximately 8-fold lower than that of acarbose under identical assay conditions. Lineweaver-Burk plot analysis indicated a mixed-type inhibition pattern, with the inhibition constant Ki calculated as 5.7 micromolar. Fluorescence quenching and circular dichroism spectroscopy revealed that EGCG binding induced a conformational change in the enzyme, reducing alpha-helical content from 42% to 31%. Molecular docking simulations showed that EGCG occupied both the catalytic site and an adjacent allosteric pocket, forming hydrogen bonds with residues Asp215, Glu277 and Arg442. These findings suggest that EGCG is a promising natural alpha-glucosidase inhibitor and provide a structural basis for the development of tea-derived functional foods targeting postprandial glucose control.
